# Extra: Full exam simulation

Claude Certified Architect – Foundations (CCAR-F) · Extra section · five videos: how it works (3:59), then four parts, one scenario each: Scenario 1, questions 1–15 (50:32); Scenario 4, questions 16–30 (49:21); Scenario 5, questions 31–45 (48:18); Scenario 6, questions 46–60 (47:11)

## Description

A full exam simulation: sixty new questions, run the way the real exam runs. The real exam gives you 120 minutes for 60 items, so every question here gets exactly two minutes on the clock. Like the exam, every question sits inside a scenario: this simulation uses four of the six, one per part, fifteen questions each. Each part opens with its scenario. Then each question is read aloud with all of its options, and the timer runs while you think and write your answer down. When the time is up, the correct answer is revealed and explained, and then every other option is taken apart, one by one, with the reason it's wrong. At each reveal you also see the question's domain, its task statement and the lesson it comes from, so you can score yourself by domain. The first video explains how the simulation works and how to score it; the next four videos are the exam itself.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the section you can:

1. Work through a full, exam-length set of scenario questions at the real exam's pace: two minutes per item.
2. Explain why the best answer is best, and why each other option fails, across all five domains.
3. Score yourself by domain and decide exactly which lessons to review next.

## How each question runs

1. **The scenario,** once at the start of each part. Read it the way you would on exam day: every question in that part builds on it.
2. **The question,** read aloud with every option. The top of the screen shows its number, its type (multiple choice: one best answer; multiple response: it says how many to choose) and its scenario.
3. **2:00 on the clock.** 120 minutes ÷ 60 items = 2 minutes per question. The timer turns amber for the last ten seconds. Write your answer down before the reveal. If you need more time, pause the video, and notice how often you need to.
4. **The answer,** with the reason it is the best one, plus the question's domain, task statement and lesson.
5. **Why not the others:** every other option, one by one, with the reason it fails. These reasons are where most of the learning is.

## The four scenarios

The exam presents four of its six scenarios, picked at random. This simulation uses Scenarios 1, 4, 5 and 6: a set none of the three practice exams uses, and one that reaches every domain.

Every multiple-response question asks you to choose two.

Each scenario tests its primary domains, as on the exam, so the domains land close to the official blueprint: Domain 1 (Agentic Architecture & Orchestration) 15 questions, Domain 2 (Tool Design & MCP Integration) 9, Domain 3 (Claude Code Configuration & Workflows) 11, Domain 4 (Prompt Engineering & Structured Output) 14, Domain 5 (Context Management & Reliability) 11. Twenty-nine of the thirty task statements appear. The one left out, 5.4 (context in large codebase exploration), doesn't fit the two scenarios here that test Domain 5; the practice exams cover it.

Every question is original, written for this course against the official blueprint. None is copied from the real exam or from the guide's sample questions, and none repeats a question from the quick checks, the module quizzes, the readiness check or the three practice exams.

## Scoring

- **Multiple choice:** one point for the right letter.
- **Multiple response:** this simulation's rule is strict: one point only when you picked every correct option, and nothing else.
- **By domain:** note the domain at every reveal, then fill in `domain-tracker.md` (the same measure as the practice-exam tracker from Lesson 8.5).
- **What it means:** the real exam reports a scaled score from 100 to 1,000, and 720 passes. A raw count from a practice set does not convert to that scale. The course's target before you book is 80% overall and 70% in every domain. Use your result to find your weakest domain, go back to its lessons, and redo their quick checks.

## In the student kit

`answer-sheet.pdf` (print it first), `answer-sheet.md`, `domain-tracker.md`, and `answer-key.md` with every scenario and question, its answer, its domain, task statement and lesson, and the reason for every option.
